The neighborhoods

Where the houses stand

Two of Philadelphia's oldest neighborhoods, a few blocks apart.

Rodman House

Society Hill

Society Hill takes its name from the Free Society of Traders, a company of investors formed in 1682 to settle William Penn's new colony. Today it holds one of the largest collections of original eighteenth-century brick homes in the country, on quiet streets a short walk from Independence National Historical Park.

Nearby: Head House Square, the old market shed and firehouse at Second and Pine; Washington Square; and the lanes and churches that still look much as they did when Philadelphia was the capital of the new nation.

Hancock House · Catherine House

Queen Village

South of South Street, Queen Village is the site of some of the region's earliest European settlement. Gloria Dei (Old Swedes') Church, completed in 1700, is one of the oldest churches in Pennsylvania. The streets here are narrower and more intimate, many still cobblestone, lined with small brick homes built for tradespeople and mariners.

Today it is one of the city's best neighborhoods for eating, with James Beard-recognized restaurants, and the Italian Market is a short walk away.
